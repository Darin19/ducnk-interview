AI Hub
I would like to start with the AI Hub project, one of the most important projects I worked on at Samsung R&D Vietnam.
AI Hub was developed based on requirements from Samsung Headquarters in Korea. I was fortunate to be trusted as the Lead Business Analyst for this project. In simple terms, AI Hub was an internal AI asset lifecycle management platform and also part of the broader Samsung AI Platform ecosystem.
The main purpose of AI Hub was to help AI teams manage datasets, AI models, training workflows, evaluation results, and related documents in a secure, standardized, and reusable way. Without this platform, datasets and models could be scattered across different teams, making it difficult to track ownership, version history, data usage, and compliance. This was especially important because using unauthorized, sensitive, copyrighted, or legally restricted datasets for AI training could create serious risks, including privacy violations, intellectual property disputes, regulatory issues, and even lawsuits.
To solve these problems, AI Hub was built with several core modules: Dataset Management, Data Source Management, Approval Workflow Management, Dataset and Model Lifecycle Status, and Permission and Access Control. Besides these core modules, the system also included Model Registry, Dataset Catalog and Search, Training Job Management, Evaluation and Benchmarking, Notification Templates, Audit Trail, and Dashboard Reporting.
With Dataset Management, each dataset had a standard Dataset Card. It stored key information such as owner, sponsor, department, data type, schema, sensitivity level, usage scope, version, labels, and related models. The system also supported dataset templates, so different types of data, such as tabular data, image data, text data, or log data, could follow the same registration standard.
Data Source Management helped control where the data came from. The source could be an internal database, API, file storage, data warehouse, external vendor, or purchased data package. Some sensitive actions, such as uploading new files, changing source connections, downloading raw data, or deleting source data, had to go through approval.
Approval Workflow Management was used to control important actions, such as asset purchase requests, dataset or model registration, sharing requests, edit or delete requests, data source approval, training job approval, and model deployment approval. The workflow supported multi-level approval, parallel approval, conditional routing, automatic approver assignment, comments, rejection, change requests, SLA tracking, and notifications.
Dataset and Model Lifecycle Status helped the system control the status of each asset, such as Draft, Under Review, Approved, Published, In Use, Deprecated, Archived, Staging, and Production. These statuses were important because they decided what users could do at each stage.
Permission and Access Control helped control who could view, edit, use, share, download, approve, or deploy AI assets. The permission logic was based on role, department, owner, sensitivity level, asset status, and usage scope.
Other modules also supported the full AI lifecycle. Model Registry helped manage model information, model versions, and related datasets. Dataset Catalog and Search helped users find reusable datasets. Training Job Management helped users request and track training jobs. Evaluation and Benchmarking helped compare model performance. Audit Trail helped track who created, edited, deleted, shared, downloaded, or approved an asset. Dashboard Reporting helped managers monitor datasets, models, approvals, and governance status.

SMART SAFE
Smart Safe is a safety and security monitoring platform using IoT technology for fire, explosion, and intrusion detection. The system integrates IoT devices, sensors (such as Gateway/Fire/Smoke/Door/PIR Motion/Glass Break), AI cameras, Google Maps, and real-time monitoring dashboards to help users detect incidents early, locate affected areas quickly, and respond in a timely manner. It supports real-time IoT monitoring of device status, connectivity, alerts, and abnormal conditions to ensure system reliability and operational safety. The system also monitors device status such as connection status, battery level, and device malfunctions in real time, and allows users to control IoT devices remotely to keep the system stable and safe. The platform also provides instant mass notification and alert mechanisms, allowing warnings to be sent to multiple users or responsible units at once. In addition, it includes flexible function-level and data-level permission management, enabling different user groups to access appropriate features, IoT device data, and monitoring scopes based on their roles and responsibilities.
The project also manages buildings where IoT devices are installed. It has been deployed to around 400 Viettel Store locations nationwide, with more than 5,000 devices installed. It helps reduce the need for on-site security guards, lower fire and intrusion risks, and is estimated to bring about VND 30 billion in value per year.
